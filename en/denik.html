2026-10-14 — Day 13: Hradec 54 → 51 after new endorsements, Deník N as a benchmark

In short: On Tuesday evening we re-ran Hradec Králové. More eliminated candidates (Holásek, Nebeská, Picek) endorsed Ceralová Petrofová. AI and AI + model now give Dvořák 51 % instead of 54 %, so he is still a narrow favourite. We also re-ran Plzeň after the row over Aschenbrenner's billboard, but the change came out below 2 points, so it stays (Řehka 82 / 94 %). Other districts unchanged. Kladno unchanged: market Paták 57 %, we Šípová 52–53 %.

News (no odds)

  • Hradec Králové (update): four eliminated candidates endorsed Ceralová Petrofová: Hůlková (ANO, 18.4 %, already counted), Nebeská (independent, 15.0 %), Holásek (outgoing senator, 12.2 %) and Picek (SPD, 5.3 %, as the "lesser evil"). Mayor Springerová backs Dvořák (she did before round 1 too). New for us are Holásek (we expected him to lean to Dvořák), Nebeská and Picek. Runoff odds: first Betting and Blend

Tipsport and Fortuna both offer a "who gets more votes" duel in all 22 districts. Method: power de-vig for each bookmaker separately, then the mean of the two. Odds of 1.01 are treated as the floor. A bookmaker with the favourite's price locked is left out. In Praha 1 (Padevět) and Kolín (Kašpar) both are locked, so Betting and Blend have no number there. In Příbram and Uherské Hradiště we use Tipsport only. Only the last reading before betting closes is scored, as pre-registered on 11 Oct. Today's numbers are interim.

  • Towards us: the market has the same favourite as AI + model in 19 of 20 districts with odds. In Hradec (Dvořák 66 %), Frýdek-Místek (Pešatová 61 %) and Praha 5 (Láska 79 %) the market sides with us against STEM (CNN Prima). In Přerov the market (Navařík 68 %) agrees with AI + model (62 %); pure AI narrowly has Vrána (51 %).
  • Away from us: in Kladno the market flipped between Sunday evening and Monday morning: Tipsport went from Šípová 1.65 / Paták 2.08 to Šípová 2.08 / Paták 1.65, and Fortuna (newly posted) has Paták at 1.65. The market now gives Paták 57 %, we have Šípová at 52–53 %, and STEM has Paták at 88 %. Blend narrowly favours Paták in Kladno (54 %). We found no news story that would explain the flip. We did not go looking for one because of the odds, so as not to change anything on the market's account.
  • The market is more confident than us (same favourite): Karviná (Brzyszkowská 87 % vs 65–72 %), Brno (Papoušek 97 % vs 84–86 %), Pelhřimov (Med 91 % vs 78–82 %), Česká Lípa (Půta 84 % vs 72–74 %), Praha 5 (79 % vs 62–64 %), Hradec (66 % vs 54 %). Close to us: Zlín (Goláň 75 % vs 73–74 %), Žďár (Klement 57 % vs 56–66 %).
  • Overall: for round 2 our forecasts look as they did in round 1. We agree with the market on the favourite but are more cautious. In round 1 that worked out better for the market on clear favourites. Kladno is now the only district where we stand against both the market and STEM.
  • Sensitivity to method: in close races the de-vig method barely matters (Kladno 56–57 % proportional / Shin / power, Hradec 63–64 %). At odds of 1.01 it matters a lot (Plzeň 95 / 97 / 99 %, Brno 91 / 94 / 97 %). The main score uses the power method, as pre-registered; the other methods will be shown at scoring as a sensitivity analysis.

2026-10-11 — Days 9–10: round-1 results, interim scores and runoff forecasts

In short: We predicted who would reach the runoff as well as the bookmakers: the favourite advanced in 26 of 27 districts in all five series. The bookmakers did better on clear favourites because they were more confident, while AI was needlessly cautious. Where we disagreed with the market, our favourite leads after round 1 in five districts and the market's in two, so the runoff will decide. Blend, the combination of AI and the market, is doing best so far. Our statistical model predicted ANO's advancement better than the Seznam Zprávy data model.

Round 1 is counted. Five seats were decided outright: Strakonice (Fiala), Děčín (Linhart), Trutnov (Sobotka), Rychnov nad Kněžnou (Grulich) and Vyškov (Grolich). The other 22 go to a runoff on 16–17 Oct. All five series forecast who wins the seat, so full scores come after round 2. For now we can only score part of it: how much probability each series put on eliminated candidates, and whether its favourite advanced. Tables are on the Scores page.

Interim scores

  • Who is still in the race: the favourite advanced in 26 of 27 districts in all five series. AI missed Plzeň (Aschenbrenner finished third); the market missed Litovel (Kohajda finished third, 146 votes short).
  • Clear favourites, market ahead: in the five outright wins the market gave the winners 88–98 %, AI 71–87 %. Brier: Betting 0.04, Blend 0.07, AI 0.36, AI + model 0.41, statistical model 0.91. AI is too cautious on clear favourites, and the statistical model even more so.
  • Disputed districts, us ahead so far: in the 7 districts where the market's favourite at the freeze differed from AI + model's, our favourite leads after round 1 in five (Cheb, Pelhřimov, Kladno, Přerov, Litovel) and the market's in two (Plzeň, Praha 5). If the round-1 leaders win the runoffs, AI + model scores a Brier of 2.7 in these districts and Betting 5.3. If the runners-up win, it flips: Betting 4.9, AI + model 7.6.
  • Overall: Blend leads on the part of the score that is already settled (Brier on eliminated candidates 0.74; Betting 0.81; AI + model 0.96; AI 1.18). The pre-registered order "Blend ≈ Betting > AI + model > statistical model ≈ AI" holds so far.
  • AI on 2 Oct and AI on 9 Oct score almost the same. A week of news barely changed the AI forecasts, because we changed them only for new facts.

Pre-registered benchmarks

  • ANO candidates advancing (Seznam Zprávy): 13 of the 23 tracked ANO candidates advanced. Seznam Zprávy expected 13.4 / 17.3 / 21.2 (unfavourable / middle / favourable scenario); our statistical model expected 14.5. Ours was closer, but it too overrated ANO. Brier on advancing: our model 0.156, Seznam Zprávy 0.188. Mean error in round-1 vote share is 3.7 pp for both.
  • Home-base hypothesis: confirmed, on a thin sample. Mayors with a big home base beat the model by 5.6 pp on average, other candidates by 1.3 pp. The 4.2 pp gap is above the pre-registered threshold of 3, but most of it comes from Kašpar in Kolín (+15); Brzyszkowská in Karviná did worse than the model (−3.3). Six candidates are not many. We take it into model v2 as a strong suspicion, not a certainty.
  • Wikipedia (most-read wins): the most-read candidate advanced or won in 14 of 27 districts. They can only go on to win where they advanced.
  • Statistical model on round-1 shares: mean error 5.3 pp per candidate.

Surprises

Dvořák (25.1 %) only went ahead once the city was counted; Ceralová Petrofová (ODS) is second. Neither we nor the market saw Nebeská coming; everyone treated her as a fringe candidate.

  1. The first version picked the right favourite in only 41 % of districts. Cause: incumbents were detected from the word “senator” in the occupation field, which is often missing. Fix: incumbency now comes from the full ČSÚ election history, including name changes (Šípová → Sucharda Šípová).
  2. In 2020 the model overrated ANO candidates by 10–15 points. Cause: a governing party gets a lower share in Senate elections than in Chamber elections, and validating without 2020 left no other cycle with ANO in government. Fix: an interaction between base and government party. There is only one such cycle in the data, so we list it as a limitation.
  3. The model underrated STAN and KDU-ČSL candidates. Cause: they are mayors' parties and do better in the Senate than their Chamber base suggests. Fix: party effects for STAN and KDU-ČSL.
  4. The model was overconfident. In the 70–90 % bucket only 32 % won. Fix: calibration on validation data. It is fitted on the same data, so it is slightly optimistic.
  5. The model couldn't see local movements (Praha sobě, SLK, SEN 21, Rozvíjíme Hradec…). Fix: a local base from municipal elections. Brier improved from 0.693 to 0.673.
  6. Artefact: the SOCDEM candidate in Karviná jumped to 43 % because of ČSSD's strength in the 2022 municipal elections, even though the party has collapsed nationally. Fix: established national parties don't count toward the local base.
  7. The Wikipedia poll table couldn't be found through search (the English site has no 2029 polling page). Fix: we took the data from Czech Wikipedia via its API. The result is an average of four pollsters instead of snippets from research.

2026-10-02 — Day 1: blind forecasts for all 27 districts

We ran the full pipeline for all 27 districts. Brno 60 and Kladno 30 had older forecasts from 21 Sep, so they got a fresh blind update. No stage saw betting odds.

Big picture: in 26 of 27 districts the favourite is an opposition candidate. The only exception is Karviná (75), where ANO defends an open seat. ANO often reaches the runoff but has a long record of losing it, especially when it is in government. The closest races are Pelhřimov (15), Litovel (66), Hradec Králové (45), Praha 5 (21) and Plzeň (9).

What went wrong and how we caught it:

  • In Plzeň, Česká Lípa, Zlín and Rychnov the red-team found a construction error. The runoff-pairing table did not match the round-1 share model; in Plzeň, for example, it put the incumbent's chance of reaching the runoff at 68 % instead of about 90 %.
  • The shared brief failed to mark 2022 as a year when ANO was in opposition. Fixed mid-run.
  • The Kladno update first came out at 55 % with no new evidence; an independent red-team pulled it to 51 %.
  • The web-search budget ran out, so most research relied on directly fetched pages.

Pre-publication check: we checked all districts against the official ČSÚ candidate register (volby.gov.cz). Every candidate the model uses is on the ballot, and the model leaves out no one significant. We did fix some labels: the Cheb incumbent is Miroslav Plevný, not Jaroslav. In Kladno, Adéla Sucharda Šípová was nominated by the Pirates on behalf of a Greens/STAN/Pirates/TOP 09/SEN 21 coalition. The site now takes names and lists straight from ČSÚ data.

Reflection: why it happened and what we do about it

  1. Construction errors in the runoff table (4 districts plus Plzeň). Cause: the model estimated runoff-pair probabilities by hand, separately from round-1 shares, and the two contradicted each other. Fix: pairings are now derived by simulation directly from round-1 shares, so consistency holds by construction. Some districts already did this. Next time it will be a mandatory deterministic script, not model judgement.
  2. An error in the shared brief (2022 not marked as an ANO-in-opposition year). Cause: one brief was sent to 24 parallel runs without a fact check, so the mistake was copied everywhere. Fix: shared base rates get an independent check before fan-out. Districts started before the fix will be checked at their next update.
  3. Unreachable web. The search budget ran out and many sources are unreachable: volby.gov.cz is a JavaScript app, iROZHLAS returned 403, regional Deník is paywalled. Research often relied on Wikipedia alone. Cause: the tool that fetches pages as text can't handle JS-rendered pages, and search has a hard per-session cap. Fix: (a) fetch ČSÚ data once, centrally, via its JSON interface into a shared cache, instead of in every district; (b) use a real browser for unreachable pages; (c) raise the search cap and plan searches up front instead of exhausting them on the first districts.
  4. Insufficient independence between stages. Because of a concurrency limit, in some districts one model did research, forecast and red-team. Fix: smaller waves instead of launching everything at once, with the red-team always a separate agent. We re-ran independent red-teams in the 4 affected districts; only one changed (Praha 5, by 3 points).
  5. “Recalibration” without new evidence (Kladno 45 → 55). Cause: the update re-ran the model on unsupported assumptions and presented it as a refinement. Fix: a new rule that any move of more than 3 points without new evidence must pass an independent red-team. Here it pulled the number back to 51.
  6. Stale forecasts. Earlier questions taught us that the biggest misses came not from a bad model but from forecasts nobody updated for weeks. Fix: a daily check of every district, even when the answer is “no change”, and a mandatory update after round 1.
